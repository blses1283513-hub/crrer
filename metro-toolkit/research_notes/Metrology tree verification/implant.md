# Implantation metrology: dopant concentration (dose, profile, junction depth) and implant damage

Scope note: this covers the "Implantation" branch of the learner's tree (parameters "Dopant concentration" and "Surface damage"). Numbers marked "typical" are values reported in a named public source for a named setup. They are not universal specs. Research date: Oct 2026.

## Q1. Which techniques are used, inline and offline, and what does each measure? (fact sheet per technique)

### Takeaway
Implant metrology works in layers. (1) The implanter measures its own dose with a Faraday cup. (2) Inline, non-destructive monitors run on monitor wafers and sometimes on product wafers: thermal-wave / photomodulated reflectance (Therma-Probe, Semilab PMR) before anneal, and four-point-probe Rs or non-contact JPV/RsL after anneal. (3) Offline lab methods give the reference profile and damage picture: SIMS (chemical dopant profile and absolute dose), SRP / ECV / differential Hall (electrically active carrier profile, junction depth), and RBS-channeling, TEM, Raman, spectroscopic ellipsometry and HRXRD (lattice damage, amorphous layer, strain).

### Cited Findings

**A. Faraday cup dosimetry (on the implanter, real time, every wafer)**
- How it works: the beam enters a Faraday cup (often placed "behind" the wafer plane so the beam falls on it as beam and wafer move relative to each other). A dose processor integrates the Faraday current over time to get total dose, and can sit in a feedback loop that controls the implanter. Corner cups can monitor dose uniformity. — [US6797967B1 patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6797967B1/en)
- Known error sources: charge exchange with residual gas. Neutralised ions still implant but are not counted, and charge stripping makes the cup over-read. Implanters therefore apply pressure compensation based on end-station pressure. In one test, mean Rs shifted by as much as 15% without compensation. — [EP0854494 Dose control patent](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.0/publication-dates/19980722/patents/EP0854494NWA2/document.html); [OSTI: pressure compensation on single-wafer implanters](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/21251647)
- The same patent says allowable tolerances on uniformity and total dose are "now at the 1% level in many applications". Note the patent dates from 1998. — [EP0854494](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.0/publication-dates/19980722/patents/EP0854494NWA2/document.html)
- Faraday cup arrays typically cover a dose sensitivity range of about 1e11 to 1e18 ions/cm². — [LLNL Faraday cup array patent, OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/874991)
- Implant tools are specified for uniformity, NOT for the accuracy of absolute dose. Absolute dose is checked by SIMS against NIST standard reference materials. — [Eurofins EAG, "Metrologies to Study Ion Implanted Semiconductor Materials", IIT 2022/JTG](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTG-1222-2-BuyuklimanliT-EurofinsEAG.pdf)
- Typical implanter dose range for Si technologies is about 5e11 to 1e16+ ions/cm². — [Coherent ion implantation glossary](https://www.coherent.com/resources/datasheet/materials/related-resources/ion-implantation/application-note-glossary-of-terms-for-ion-implantation-and-related-processes.pdf)

**B. Thermal-wave / modulated photo-reflectance (KLA Therma-Probe; "TW units")**
- How it works: an intensity-modulated pump laser excites thermal and plasma (carrier) waves in the Si. A probe laser measures the modulated change in reflectance. The signal tracks lattice damage, and software correlates it to dose. — [US6671047B2 patent (Therma-Wave)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6671047B2/en); [Therma-Wave 424(b) prospectus, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/828119/000089161803006437/f93967b1e424b1.htm)
- Non-contact and non-damaging, so it can measure product wafers right after implant, with no anneal or other processing. Early work reported dose measurement down to the 1e10 ions/cm² range. — [Smith 1987, NIM B](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0168583X87908998); [SEC prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/828119/000089161803006437/f93967b1e424b1.htm)
- Limitation at high dose: once amorphisation sets in, the TW signal is no longer monotonic with dose and cannot reliably monitor the implant. Optical methods (SE, spectroscopic reflectance) are then used to measure the amorphous-layer thickness. — [US6671047B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6671047B2/en)
- Mid-dose sensitivity dip: dose sensitivity (% change in TW / % change in dose) plotted against TW value is "V"-shaped, with a minimum near 600–700 TW units. In an IIT-2000 matrix (B 5e11–1e14, 3–500 keV), the minimum sensitivity ranged from 0.083 at 3 keV to 0.223 at 2 MeV. — [IIT 2000, IEEE 924243](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/924243)
- Signal also depends on channeling, dose rate and time since implant (relaxation), surface condition and measurement parameters. Heavy ions (In, Sb) were shown to be sensitive to implant conditions and wafer surface status. — [Therma-Probe 150 study, NIM B 1987](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0168583X87909013); [IEEE 1257985 (In/Sb)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1257985); [Kamenitsa 1993, sources of variation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0168583X9395050F)
- Current products: KLA describes Therma-Probe 680XP (2X/1X nm nodes) and 780 (<1X nm) as inline implant/anneal metrology. Outputs: dose, energy and angle-of-incidence (AOI), implant and anneal uniformity micro-maps, end-of-range damage, and anneal activation / damage recovery. The 780 also supports SiC/GaN. Uses: inline process monitor, tool monitor, process tool matching. — [KLA Therma-Probe product listing (DirectIndustry mirror)](https://www.directindustry.com/prod/kla-corporation/product-113449-1595647.html); [KLA metrology page mirror](https://microsense.net/about)
- KLA's May 2026 SEC filing still lists the "Therma-Probe Series" alongside "OmniMap RS-xxx Series" (sheet resistance) and the "CAPRES product family" in chip-manufacturing metrology. — [KLA SD filing 05/26/2026](https://ir.kla.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000319201-26-000020/0000319201-26-000020.pdf)
- Annealed USJ mode: the TP signal on annealed ultra-shallow junctions varied linearly with junction depth. Correlation to SIMS was better than 0.98. Long-term repeatability was <0.3% (1σ), typically 0.16%. Energy resolution was 1–10 eV and depth resolution 0.4–1.8 Å (B and BF2, 1e14–1e15, 200–1000 eV, spike anneal). — [Bakshi et al., Therma-Wave, ECS 2002](https://www.electrochem.org/dl/ma/201/pdfs/0738.pdf)

**C. Photomodulated reflectance / Carrier Illumination (Semilab PMR-3000)**
- PMR monitors as-implanted, pre-anneal production wafers with a spot smaller than 3 µm. The signal depends on both implant damage and carrier-concentration change, so it must be calibrated with dose ladders. Typical figures: dose range 5e10 to 5.5e16 ions/cm², dose detectability <0.5% (1σ), tilt-angle resolution about 0.1° (1σ), and signal repeatability 3σ <0.15% on the reference wafer. Measure before anneal, because anneal removes the damage and sensitivity "significantly decreases". The redesign was claimed to remove the mid-dose (5e13–5e14) insensitive region. — [Semilab PMR paper, hosted by Axcelis](https://www.axcelis.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Photomodulated-Reflectance-Measurement-Technique-for-Implantation-Tilt-Angle-Monitoring.pdf)

**D. Four-point probe sheet resistance (Rs) after anneal (e.g., KLA OmniMap/RS, R54; legacy Prometrix RS-55, Tencor RS100)**
- Contact method. The implant layer must be annealed to activate the dopant and form the contact before 4PP measurement, which is done on the implant monitor wafer. With careful sample preparation and a conditioned probe head, reliable performance is expected down to about 1e12 ions/cm². Probe qualification is usually to <0.2%. Rs maps reveal implant shifts and anneal problems, e.g. higher Rs when the anneal does not reach the temperature needed for full activation. — [AZoM / KLA Instruments, 2025](https://www.azom.com/article.aspx?ArticleID=24723)
- Rs is "an industry standard for monitoring high and medium dose ion implants". Careful processing extended direct Rs down to about 2e11 ions/cm². The double-implant technique extends Rs monitoring to about 5e9 ions/cm². — [Smith & Johnson, "Sheet resistance monitoring of low dose implants using the double implant technique"](https://www.academia.edu/105022723/Sheet_resistance_monitoring_of_low_dose_implants_using_the_double_implant_technique)
- Semilab: Rs "varies with dose, energy, and the amount of implanted species that has become electrically active", so it monitors implant and anneal together. — [Semilab sheet resistance page](https://semilab.com/category/products/sheet-resistance-measurement-1)
- Ultra-high-energy implants (deep image-sensor photodiodes): conventional 4PP is "inappropriate" because the dopant sits deep. A sacrificial-oxide method is used instead. — [MRS Advances 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1557/s43580-022-00365-5)

**E. Non-contact junction photovoltage (JPV) / RsL (Semilab; formerly also Frontier Semiconductor)**
- How it works: chopped LED light creates carriers that the p-n junction separates. The junction photovoltage spreads laterally in the implanted layer and is picked up by a capacitive probe at several modulation frequencies. From this the tool extracts Rs, junction capacitance Cd and diode resistance Rd, and leakage current I_L = kT/q / Rd. Calibration uses wafers verified with 4PP. — [Semilab non-contact Rs page](https://semilab.com/category/products/non-contact-sheet-resistance-measurement); [Semilab Rs page](https://semilab.com/category/products/sheet-resistance-measurement-0)
- JPV is an alternative to 4PP that is done after anneal. It is especially useful for USJ (no probe punch-through) and is sensitive to activated species. Example: 300 mm wafer, 1 mm raster, about 70k points at about 1k points/min. A map of B 1 keV 4e14 showed the 4-fold symmetry of a quad implant at 22°, revealing twist/tilt, with Rs variation of ±2%. — [Semilab, Junction Technology Group 2007](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/JTG2007/JTG_2007_07_9Kohn.pdf)
- RsL also measures leakage current on unpatterned monitor wafers, which makes leakage usable as a USJ process-control parameter. — [Faifer, Current et al. 2006 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Forward-and-reverse-bias-leakage-currents-from-I-V-measurements-for-10-keV_fig2_260513196); [Non-contact Rs and leakage for USJ/SDE/Halo](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4224992_Non-contact_measurement_of_sheet_resistance_and_leakage_current_Applications_for_USJ-SDEHalo_junctions)

**F. SIMS (dynamic SIMS; e.g., Cameca magnetic sector; EAG service)**
- How it works: O2+ or Cs+ primary ions sputter the sample, and the secondary ions are mass-analysed. Detection limits are about 1e10–1e16 atoms/cm³ depending on element. Depth resolution >5 Å, dynamic range up to 6 orders, lateral probe ≥10 µm in depth-profiling mode. Listed uses include ultra-shallow implant profiling and "high-precision matching of process tools such as ion implanters". Destructive (sputter crater). — [EAG SIMS technique page](https://www.eag.com/techniques/mass-spec/secondary-ion-mass-spectrometry-sims)
- Why implant needs SIMS: profile shape and dose, tool matching, energy impurities, surface and implanted elemental impurities, and annealing development. HPIC protocol results: 0.17% RSD on total dose (5 pieces × 5 loads × 3 repeats), and dose differences <2% measurable. Absolute dose for B, P and As in Si is traceable to NIST SRMs. — [EAG IIT 2022 deck](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTG-1222-2-BuyuklimanliT-EurofinsEAG.pdf)
- SIMS gives total (chemical) concentration and cannot separate active from inactive dopant. — [IOP Semicond. Sci. Technol. 2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6641/ac0854)
- Shallow and high-concentration implants need special protocols (e.g., EAG "PCOR-SIMS"). Example: plasma-doped poly-Si dose was NRA 6.90e16 vs PCOR-SIMS 7.14e16, but 1.54e17 with conventional SIMS. — [EAG IIT 2022 deck](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTG-1222-2-BuyuklimanliT-EurofinsEAG.pdf)
- NIST SRM 2133 (31P implanted at 100 keV) is certified for retained dose. Rs mapping of a companion wafer showed 0.43% RSD over 81 points. — [NIST SRM 2133 certificate](https://tsapps.nist.gov/srmext/certificates/2133.pdf)
- Throughput example: an optimised deep H (proton) profile took about 2 h. — [ScienceDirect 2024, proton implant SIMS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S136980012400115X)

**G. Spreading resistance profiling (SRP / SRA)**
- How it works: the sample is angle-lapped, and two closely spaced probes step down the bevel at about 5 mV. Calibration standards plus mathematics give resistivity vs depth and carrier concentration vs depth. It covers about 8 decades of resistivity/concentration and can profile >500 µm deep. Carrier concentration in poly is under-reported because mobility is lower. — [Solecon Labs tech note "SRP and SIMS"](https://soleconstage.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/srp-sims.pdf)
- Bevels are ground with 0.1 or 0.05 µm diamond paste at angles of about 0.001–0.2 rad. Carrier concentration can vary by up to 10 orders of magnitude. — [Wikipedia: Spreading resistance profiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spreading_resistance_profiling)
- Destructive (grinding). Si only at that lab. Structures need to be at least about 100 × 400 µm. — [SGS Institut Fresenius SRP](https://sgs-institut-fresenius.de/en/material-failure-analysis/measuring-and-analysis-methods/physical-surface-analysis/spreading-resistance-profiling)
- Sensitivity down to about 1e12 cm⁻³ and "virtually unlimited probing depth", but SRP needs dopant activation and gives no as-implanted profile. — [ScienceDirect 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S136980012400115X)
- SRP shows only net carrier concentration, not the separate p- and n-type dopants. SIMS shows those. — [EAG IIT 2022 deck](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTG-1222-2-BuyuklimanliT-EurofinsEAG.pdf)
- USJ SRP needs a Poisson solver. Bevel-based methods become "extremely difficult" at the resolution ultrathin layers need. — [SPIE 4227](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/4227/0000/Spreading-resistance-profiling-of-ultrashallow-junction-NPN-BJT-with-carrier/10.1117/12.405373.full); [Differential Hall paper, PMC 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6036972)

**H. Electrochemical C-V (ECV) and differential Hall effect (DHE)**
- ECV: the Schottky contact is made through an electrolyte that also etches. The profile goes deeper by etching rather than by increasing bias. — [UCF ECS abstract](https://ecs.confex.com/ecs/224/webprogram/Abstract/Paper25304/A1-0130.pdf)
- WEP CVP21 spec: concentration <1e12 to >1e21 cm⁻³, depth 1 nm to 100 µm, samples from 4×2 mm² up to 8" wafer. Group IV and III-V. — [NMTronics / WEP CVP21](https://www.nmtronics.com/solar/wep.html); [NISE](https://nise.res.in/departments/advanced-solar-cell-characterization-laboratory)
- ECV gives electrically active concentration, but etch depth must be measured separately. Profiles of as-implanted (amorphous) material are not interpretable. After activation the ECV profile matched SIMS (active peak 1.59e20 vs SIMS 4.8e20 B). — [IOP 2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6641/ac0854)
- Capacitance methods (ECV, SCM) are reliable only when there are no additional electrically active defects. DHE gives sub-nm-resolution active-dopant and mobility profiles for ultrathin layers. EAG lists DHE for active-dopant profiling of <10 nm S/D P layers. — [PMC 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6036972); [EAG IIT 2022 deck](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTG-1222-2-BuyuklimanliT-EurofinsEAG.pdf)

**I. Damage / amorphisation / strain methods (lab, mostly destructive or slow)**
- RBS-channeling (RBS/C): a beam aligned with a crystal axis sees reduced scattering. Displaced lattice atoms in the damaged zone raise the aligned yield and cause dechanneling, which quantifies crystal damage and its recovery after anneal. — [EAG IIT 2022 deck](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTG-1222-2-BuyuklimanliT-EurofinsEAG.pdf)
- An automated RBS/C system took about 20–30 min per sample, and damage depth profiles agreed with SRIM. — [PubMed 11999162](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11999162)
- Displaced-atom fraction at the damage peak rises with fluence up to about 90%. Full amorphisation is identified when the channeling spectrum reaches the random level. — [INIS RBS/Raman/TEM study](https://inis.iaea.org/records/fj845-tne62/files/48098816.pdf)
- Cross-section TEM: images defect bands and amorphous layers. SIMS and TEM together showed dopant and oxygen decoration of dislocation layers after anneal. — [EAG IIT 2022 deck](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTG-1222-2-BuyuklimanliT-EurofinsEAG.pdf)
- TEM showed 0.5–1 keV B did not amorphise Si, but the implanted layer was still visible because of strain. — [Ion Implantation (InTech 2012), SE chapter](http://www.issp.ac.ru/ebooks/books/open/Ion_Implantation.pdf)
- XTEM plus RBS were used to study secondary-defect (dislocation loop) bands that lie slightly deeper than Rp. — [Chinese Physics, self-ion implanted Si](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1004-423X/2/11/005)
- Spectroscopic ellipsometry (SE): characterises implanted crystalline substrates before anneal, whether the damaged layer is amorphised (higher energy) or just strained. It uses optical models such as Tauc-Lorentz for a-Si. — [Ion Implantation (InTech 2012)](http://www.issp.ac.ru/ebooks/books/open/Ion_Implantation.pdf)
- Optical methods "can unambiguously measure the thickness of the amorphous layer" in the high-dose regime where thermal waves fail. Combining TW with spectroscopy covers the whole dose range. — [US6671047B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6671047B2/en)
- Early work: the ellipsometric psi correlated with degree of amorphousness, cross-checked with RBS/C. — [Lohner et al. 1981, INIS](https://inis.iaea.org/records/91h2f-e8658)
- Raman: c-Si gives a sharp band at about 521 cm⁻¹ and a-Si a broad band at about 480 cm⁻¹. The peak ratio estimates the crystalline/amorphous fraction. — [Thermo Fisher app note](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/CAD/Application-Notes/D16998~.pdf)
- Raman plus XTEM tracked how amorphisation in Si-implanted Si (200 keV, 5e14) depends on implant temperature. — [JJAP 30, 3617](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1143/JJAP.30.3617)
- HRXRD: XRD (Servidori 1987) was shown to be a powerful structural diagnostic of implant damage. ω/2θ profiles of H-implanted Si were shown as implanted and after anneal. — [Ion Implantation (InTech 2012)](http://www.issp.ac.ru/ebooks/books/open/Ion_Implantation.pdf)
- In 4H-SiC and 6H-SiC, HRXRD gives strain depth profiles (up to about 2% max strain following the dpa profile) alongside RBS/C and Raman. — [Applied Surface Science 2024 (4H-SiC)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169433224010092); [ORNL 6H-SiC](https://www.ornl.gov/publications/investigation-of-irradiation-effects-induced-by-self-ion-in-6h-sic-combining-rbs-c-raman-and-xrd/)
- Pre-amorphisation implants (PAI) use Si or Ge at mid-to-high dose (about 5–8e14) to suppress channeling. These are the main implants whose amorphous-layer depth is measured. — [Coherent glossary](https://www.coherent.com/resources/datasheet/materials/related-resources/ion-implantation/application-note-glossary-of-terms-for-ion-implantation-and-related-processes.pdf)

### Inferences
- A clean tree split is: **Dose/uniformity monitors (inline)**: Faraday (tool), TW/PMR (pre-anneal), Rs-4PP / JPV (post-anneal). **Profile / junction depth (lab)**: SIMS (chemical), SRP / ECV / DHE (electrical). **Damage (pre-anneal inline + lab)**: TW/PMR (damage-based signal), SE (amorphous thickness), Raman, RBS/C, XTEM, HRXRD (strain).
- "Surface damage" in the learner's notes is better labelled "implant (lattice) damage / amorphisation". The damage sits throughout the implanted range down to end-of-range, not only at the surface (see TEM and end-of-range sources above).

## Q2. Technique attributes: destructive or not, contact or not, monitor vs product wafer, inline vs lab, sensitivity, throughput, typical use

### Takeaway
Only the Faraday cup (every wafer, in-situ) and the optical pump-probe methods (TW/PMR) can be used directly on product wafers. Rs-4PP and JPV need an annealed blanket monitor wafer (4PP also touches the wafer). SIMS, SRP, ECV, TEM and RBS are destructive or slow lab references used for calibration, tool matching and excursion analysis.

| HRXRD | Strain depth profile from damage/dopant | No | No | Coupon/wafer | Lab | Max strain ~2% in SiC study ([ORNL](https://www.ornl.gov/publications/investigation-of-irradiation-effects-induced-by-self-ion-in-6h-sic-combining-rbs-c-raman-and-xrd/)) | Damage/strain research, SiC |

Additional attribute facts:
- TW and PMR must be measured before anneal: anneal removes the damage the signal relies on, and sensitivity drops sharply afterwards. — [Semilab PMR](https://www.axcelis.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Photomodulated-Reflectance-Measurement-Technique-for-Implantation-Tilt-Angle-Monitoring.pdf)
- 4PP needs anneal to activate the ions and form the contact. — [AZoM/KLA](https://www.azom.com/article.aspx?ArticleID=24723)
- Low-dose monitors drift with time, "within minutes of an implant or within days or months". — [NIM B 1991, "Daily 2×10^12 monitoring of an ion implanter"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0168583X9196157G)
- Rs is temperature-sensitive: a fab temperature swing of 4–8 °C with TCR 0.8%/°C gives a 3.2–6.4% Rs variation. — [Smith & Johnson](https://www.academia.edu/105022723/Sheet_resistance_monitoring_of_low_dose_implants_using_the_double_implant_technique) (the source text printed "O.S%/°C", an OCR garble; 0.8%/°C is the value consistent with the stated 3.2–6.4% for 4–8 °C)
- Implanter wafer temperature also shifts post-anneal Rs. In an Axcelis study of 7 keV B 4.5e15, Rs at 18 °C cooling was about 3.5% lower than at 32 °C, and this could not be compensated by dose. — [Axcelis paper](https://www.axcelis.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/axcelis_integrationborontempcontrol.pdf)

### Inferences
- Throughput order (inferred from the above): Faraday (every wafer, real time) > TW/PMR and JPV (minutes per map, no prep) > 4PP (minutes, but needs anneal first) >> RBS/C (~0.5 h) > SIMS (hours per profile plus lab queue) ≈ SRP/ECV (sample prep) > TEM (FIB prep).
- For the learner tree: label TW/PMR "pre-anneal, damage-based, non-contact, can be product"; Rs-4PP "post-anneal, contact, monitor wafer"; JPV "post-anneal, non-contact, monitor wafer with junction".

### Gaps
- No public, consistent throughput figures (wafers/hour) were found for current TW, 4PP or JPV tools. Only the Semilab JPV mapping rate (~1k points/min) is sourced.
- No public precision figure for routine Raman implant monitoring was found.

## Q3. Typical implant monitoring practice: monitor cadence, Rs dose sensitivity, why TW before anneal and Rs after, angle (tilt/twist) checks, energy contamination

### Takeaway
Fabs qualify each implanter with blanket monitor wafers. Monitors are read pre-anneal by TW/PMR (fast, non-contact, damage-based) and/or post-anneal by Rs (electrically meaningful, includes activation), and SIMS is the periodic reference for absolute dose and tool matching. Rs dose sensitivity is about 1 (%Rs per %dose) at low dose and falls when activation saturates. TW sensitivity is "V"-shaped with a weak mid-dose zone. Angle is monitored with channeling-sensitive implants, where the TW/PMR/Rs signal changes strongly with tilt/twist (V-curves). Energy contamination (decel, energetic metals) is caught by SIMS.

### Cited Findings
**Monitor cadence and practice**
- Implant tolerances on uniformity and total dose are at the 1% level in many applications, which drives frequent monitoring. — [EP0854494](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.0/publication-dates/19980722/patents/EP0854494NWA2/document.html)
- Daily low-dose (2e12) implanter monitoring is documented as a practice, along with its drift problem. — [NIM B 1991](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0168583X9196157G)
- A patented method implants monitor wafers immediately after a production lot (loaded in the cassette after it) to test profile, dose placement and accuracy. A "qual" angle of about 36° deliberately aligns a crystal channel with the beam, so angle errors show up strongly. — [US20090166564A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20090166564A1/en)
- Classic workflow: test wafers are implanted, annealed, and Rs is measured by 4PP. Measured Rs differences are converted to dose differences using a characterised dose–Rs curve for the given energy and anneal. — [NIM B 1993, "The relationship between sheet resistance and ion implant dose"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0168583X9395055A)
- TW is also correlated against 4PP Rs on test wafers when qualifying it as a production implant monitor. — [NIM B 1991, TW of high-dose implants](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0168583X9196266N)
- Example qualification recipe (Axcelis): TW measured as implanted and Rs after RTP (1040 °C, 9 s) on the same wafers, with SIMS on selected wafers. — [Axcelis paper](https://www.axcelis.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/axcelis_integrationborontempcontrol.pdf)

**Dose sensitivity**
- Sensitivity is defined as % change in measured value / % change in monitored value. Direct Rs has sensitivity "about unity in the low dose region". Double-implant Rs can reach up to 1.5. — [Smith & Johnson](https://www.academia.edu/105022723/Sheet_resistance_monitoring_of_low_dose_implants_using_the_double_implant_technique)
- TW sensitivity is "V"-shaped against TW value, with a minimum of 0.083 (3 keV) to 0.223 (2 MeV) near 500–700 TW units. Near that minimum, dose variations are hard to see. — [IIT 2000](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/924243)
- Saturation at high dose: for B 1 keV, higher dose did not change Rs (map deviation flat up to 4e15), while for As higher dose did lower Rs. B maps were dominated by the implanter signature and As maps by the anneal. — [Semilab JTG 2007](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/JTG2007/JTG_2007_07_9Kohn.pdf)
- SIMS (HPIC) can resolve dose differences <2% with 0.17% RSD. — [EAG deck](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTG-1222-2-BuyuklimanliT-EurofinsEAG.pdf)

**Why TW before anneal, Rs after**
- TW/PMR signals come from lattice damage (and carriers), which anneal removes. So they are measured as-implanted, immediately, with no processing, and can run on product wafers. — [Semilab PMR](https://www.axcelis.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Photomodulated-Reflectance-Measurement-Technique-for-Implantation-Tilt-Angle-Monitoring.pdf); [SEC prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/828119/000089161803006437/f93967b1e424b1.htm); [Smith 1987](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0168583X87908998)
- Rs needs electrically active dopant, so anneal comes first. It reflects dose, energy and activation, which "ultimately determines the device performance". — [Semilab](https://semilab.com/category/products/sheet-resistance-measurement-1); [AZoM/KLA](https://www.azom.com/article.aspx?ArticleID=24723)
- TW also works after anneal in an annealed-USJ mode, with the pre-anneal measurement used to select the calibration curve. — [ECS 2002](https://www.electrochem.org/dl/ma/201/pdfs/0738.pdf)

**Tilt/twist (angle) monitoring**
- Definitions: tilt is the angle between wafer normal and beam. Twist is the azimuthal angle relative to the primary flat/notch. Non-zero tilt avoids channeling. — [TU Wien, Hössinger thesis](https://www.iue.tuwien.ac.at/phd/hoessinger/node24.html)
- About 7° tilt is generally the best compromise for avoiding channeling. — [Ion Implantation (InTech 2012)](http://www.issp.ac.ru/ebooks/books/open/Ion_Implantation.pdf)
- Very high energy implants: channeling tails differ for tilt changes as small as 0.04° at tilts ≤0.25°. The TW value was higher at 1.0° than at 0.5° tilt. The wafer cut offset was verified with the TW "V-curve" (TW vs tilt) and corrected in the tool angle. Certified wafers had cut offset <0.05°. — [Axcelis IIT paper 2018/2019](https://www.axcelis.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Analysis-of-Very-High-Energy-Implantation-Profiles-at-Channeling-and-Non-Channeling-Conditions.pdf); [Axcelis 2018 version](https://www.axcelis.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Analysis-of-Very-High-Energy-Implant-Profiles-at-Channeling-and-Non-Channeling-Conditions.pdf)
- PMR detects a tilt variation of about 0.1° (1σ) on B 50 keV 5e13 wafers swept from −2° to +2°. — [Semilab PMR](https://www.axcelis.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Photomodulated-Reflectance-Measurement-Technique-for-Implantation-Tilt-Angle-Monitoring.pdf)
- JPV Rs maps show quad-implant symmetry at 22°, revealing twist/tilt. Batch implanters leave arcs (8.3 mm pitch). — [Semilab JTG 2007](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/JTG2007/JTG_2007_07_9Kohn.pdf)
- Beam divergence also affects medium-dose channeling implants. — [MRS Advances, SMIT SAion](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1557/s43580-022-00486-x)

**Energy contamination and metals**
- SIMS detects energy contamination from deceleration-mode shallow B implants (needs high depth resolution, high dynamic range, low memory). It also detects energetic Al contamination in a P implant: Al (<200 Å) 3.54e12 and (>200 Å) 8.57e11 atoms/cm². — [EAG deck](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTG-1222-2-BuyuklimanliT-EurofinsEAG.pdf)
- Metrology sensitivity to energy shifts of ±20 eV around As 2 keV was studied with LEXES and SIMS. — [INIS 2005](https://inis.iaea.org/records/y01w5-jfv12)

### Inferences
- A defensible teaching statement is: "%ΔRs ≈ −%Δdose (sensitivity ~1) at low/medium dose. It falls toward 0 when activation is solubility-limited (high-dose B), and also depends on anneal and wafer temperature." This is derived from Smith & Johnson plus Semilab JTG 2007.
- TW and Rs complement each other. TW gives fast, product-compatible, pre-anneal feedback (catching implanter faults before anneal). Rs confirms the electrical result after anneal and also catches anneal faults. SIMS arbitrates absolute dose and tool matching, because implanters are not specified for absolute dose.

### Gaps
- No public source gives a standard cadence such as "one monitor per implanter per shift". Cadence is fab-specific. Public evidence only supports "daily" monitors and monitors run after production lots. Teach it as "typically daily or per shift / after PM and recipe change (fab-specific)", and flag it as unsourced beyond the daily example.
- No public source found that gives a single industry-typical TW sensitivity in the high-sensitivity (low-dose) arm. Only the minimum values are sourced.

## Q4. Confirm or correct: "sheet resistance (4-point probe) applies to implant films"

### Takeaway
**Confirmed, with a condition.** 4PP Rs is a standard implant monitor, but only after an activation anneal on a blanket monitor wafer, where the implanted layer forms a junction or isolating layer to the substrate. As-implanted wafers are monitored with TW/PMR instead. Special cases (USJ probe penetration, very low dose, ultra-high energy) need precautions or alternatives (JPV, double-implant, sacrificial oxide).

### Cited Findings
- "To create the connection and activate the ions, an anneal is necessary before four-point probe measurements, which can be performed on the implant monitor wafer." USJ layers "may necessitate particular precautions to reduce probe penetration." — [AZoM / KLA Instruments 2025](https://www.azom.com/article.aspx?ArticleID=24723)
- "Sheet resistance has become an industry standard for monitoring high and medium dose ion implants." — [Smith & Johnson](https://www.academia.edu/105022723/Sheet_resistance_monitoring_of_low_dose_implants_using_the_double_implant_technique)
- "The combination of the ion implant process and the process to anneal implants is usually monitored by measuring the sheet resistance of the implanted layer." — [Semilab](https://semilab.com/category/products/sheet-resistance-measurement-1)
- 4PP is valid if conditions are met: large area, top layer only, uniform resistivity, and an insulator or electrical junction underneath. — [Solecon "SRP and SIMS"](https://soleconstage.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/srp-sims.pdf)
- For ultra-high-energy implants, conventional 4PP is inappropriate and a sacrificial-oxide method is used. — [MRS Advances 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1557/s43580-022-00365-5)
- JPV is a non-contact alternative after anneal, "especially useful for USJ". — [Semilab JTG 2007](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/JTG2007/JTG_2007_07_9Kohn.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording for the tree: "Sheet resistance (4PP, contact; or JPV, non-contact) — measured after activation anneal on blanket implant monitor wafers (needs a junction or insulating layer under the doped layer). It reflects dose × activation × junction depth. Use TW/PMR for as-implanted (pre-anneal) dose and damage."
- Suggested additions to the learner's two parameters: under **Dopant concentration**: Faraday dose (in-tool), Rs-4PP/JPV (post-anneal), TW/PMR (pre-anneal), SIMS (profile/absolute dose/Xj), SRP/ECV/DHE (active profile/electrical Xj). Under **Implant damage (rename from "Surface damage")**: TW/PMR, SE (amorphous thickness), Raman, RBS/C, XTEM, HRXRD.

### Gaps
- None material to the confirm/correct question.
