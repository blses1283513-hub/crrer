## Objections

OBJECTION 1
- type: missing-field-knowledge
- target step: Objection 7 / 修訂版結論 step 7 — "儀式要求的結構性代價...能預測制度性存續延長...移入 relevant" (costly signaling as a general relevant factor for institutional persistence)
- failure case: Sosis & Bressler (2003) found the cost–longevity correlation held **only for religiously-framed communes**; for secular communes, mean duration never exceeded ~10 years regardless of how many costly requirements were imposed. The revision states the costly-signaling finding as a general mechanism ("能預測制度性存續延長") without carrying over this boundary condition. Many of the festival customs the question is actually about (e.g. civic/secular holidays, non-religious family gatherings) sit closer to the "secular" side of Sosis's own data, where his effect was reported as absent. Applying the claim unqualified risks over-generalizing a religion-specific empirical result to the general "節慶習俗" case the model is meant to explain.
- source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1069397103037002003 (Sosis & Bressler 2003, "Cooperation and Commune Longevity: A Test of the Costly Signaling Theory of Religion", Cross-Cultural Research) — abstract/summary confirmed via search aggregation; direct full-text fetch was blocked (403/binary-PDF), so treat this as search-verified rather than full-text-verified.
- severity: major
- would resolve it: restate the factor as "structural ritual cost predicts institutional persistence, conditional on the practice carrying (or being embeddable in) a religious/sacred framing — the effect was not observed for secular institutions in the source study"; explicitly flag that for purely secular festival customs, costly-signaling should not yet be assumed to transfer, pending a secular-specific test.
